How many bilayers make up the mitochondrial double membrane?	Two separate bilayers, with an intermembrane space between them (four leaflets total).
Does the nuclear envelope contain one bilayer or two?	Two bilayers: the inner and outer nuclear membranes. The outer membrane is continuous with the ER.
Name a course organelle surrounded by a single membrane bilayer.	ER, Golgi, or lysosome.
What protein framework helps maintain cell shape?	The cytoskeleton.
What three claims make up cell theory?	Organisms consist of cells; the cell is the fundamental unit of life; cells arise from pre-existing cells.
Why is a collection of isolated macromolecules not yet a living cell?	A cell integrates a compartment, information processing, energy use, growth/division and responses to its environment.
How does a unicellular organism differ from one cell in a multicellular organism?	The single cell is the whole organism; a cell in a multicellular organism is one cooperating, often specialized part.
Which three domains contain cellular organisms?	Bacteria, Archaea and Eukarya.
Can a cell’s size alone establish whether it is bacterial or eukaryotic?	No. Size ranges overlap; evaluate cellular organization and multiple features.
Estimate the long-axis length of this cell using its 20 μm scale bar.	About 160 μm: the cell spans eight scale-bar lengths.
Which basic components occur in both the bacterial and eukaryotic models?	A plasma membrane, cytoplasm, DNA and ribosomes.
The DNA region marked A has no surrounding nuclear envelope. What is this region in the bacterial model?	The nucleoid.
What separates nuclear DNA from the cytoplasm in the eukaryotic model?	A nuclear envelope; bacterial nucleoid DNA is not enclosed by that envelope.
What membrane arrangement makes the lecture’s E. coli example Gram-negative?	It has an inner plasma membrane and an outer membrane.
Why does finding ribosomes not establish that a cell is eukaryotic?	Both bacterial and eukaryotic cells use ribosomes for protein synthesis.
How do genome, chromosome and gene relate to one another?	A genome is the complete genetic information; a chromosome is a DNA molecule with associated organization; a gene is a functional DNA sequence within it.
Where are the two genomes described for yeast located?	In the nucleus and in mitochondria.
Compare the lecture examples: E. coli chromosome versus yeast nuclear chromosomes.	E. coli: a circular chromosome; yeast nucleus: 16 linear chromosomes.
Which is larger in the lecture examples: E. coli’s genome or yeast’s nuclear genome?	Yeast’s nuclear genome: about 12.1 Mb versus E. coli’s 4.6 Mb.
Why do organelle DNA and ribosomes support a bacterial origin of mitochondria and chloroplasts?	They retain bacterial-like genetic features: their own genomes, bacterial-related sequences and bacterial-like protein-synthesis machinery.
What organelle replication behavior supports endosymbiosis?	Mitochondria and chloroplasts grow and divide by fission rather than being built from nothing at each host-cell division.
Match the lecture’s bacterial ancestors to their organelles.	Alphaproteobacteria → mitochondria; cyanobacteria → plastids.
Does being a single cell make yeast a bacterium?	No. Yeast is a unicellular eukaryote with a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.
A bacterial cell increases in size without dividing. Has the population’s cell number increased?	No. Individual growth increases cell size; division increases cell number.
What is the distinction between mitosis and cytokinesis in the lecture?	Mitosis divides the nucleus; cytokinesis divides the cytoplasm into daughter cells.
Why grow cells in laboratory nutrient medium?	To maintain and expand cells under controlled conditions so their processes can be studied.
Why does a heterotrophic cell need nutrients such as glucose and amino acids in its medium?	They supply usable carbon/building materials; energy-yielding nutrients also support cellular work and synthesis.
On a log₂ cell-density axis, what does an increase of one vertical unit mean?	Cell density doubles.
Which phase fits cells adjusting gene expression after transfer to fresh medium, with little net population increase?	Lag phase.
What does the straight rising segment on this logarithmic growth plot represent?	Exponential (log) growth: cells are added by division faster than they are lost.
Does a stationary-phase plateau mean that every cell has stopped dividing?	No. The viable population is roughly constant; some cells divide, die or stop dividing.
What balance of division and death explains a falling viable-cell-density curve?	Cell loss by death exceeds addition by division.
In the lecture’s seven-phase curve, what do regions 2, 4 and 7 add to the four main phases?	Acceleration, deceleration and long-term stationary phase, respectively.
Why can a closed nutrient culture stop increasing even when some cells remain alive?	Nutrients become limiting and metabolic wastes accumulate.
Why can lag-phase cells be metabolically active despite little increase in cell number?	They adjust gene expression and make the proteins needed to use the new environment.
Why must chromosome replication accompany repeated bacterial population doubling?	Each daughter cell needs a copy of the genetic instructions.
From these sugar curves, which substrate is consumed first?	Glucose: its concentration falls before xylose begins to decline.
Why is plateau i interpreted as a second lag phase rather than the final stationary phase?	Glucose is exhausted, but xylose remains; cells adjust metabolism and then resume exponential growth.
Why does plateau ii indicate stationary phase in this two-sugar experiment?	Both sugars are depleted; the population no longer increases.
Why can ¹⁴C-labelled glucose reveal where glucose-derived carbon goes?	Its radioactive carbon atoms can be detected after incorporation into cellular products.
Does taking up labelled glucose restrict its carbon to carbohydrates?	No. In the lecture experiment its carbon appears in CO₂, DNA, proteins, lipids and cell-wall polysaccharides.
A molecule purified from cells contains the labelled α-carbon from alanine. What does that label establish?	That alanine-derived carbon entered the product; it does not by itself identify the whole product or pathway.
A culture undergoes three doublings from 2 × 10⁴ cells/mL with negligible death. What density results?	1.6 × 10⁵ cells/mL: 2 × 10⁴ × 2³.
