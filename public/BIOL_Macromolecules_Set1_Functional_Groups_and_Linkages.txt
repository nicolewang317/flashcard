In a structural formula, what does R usually represent?	An unspecified organic group or the rest of the molecule.
In R–C(=O)–O–R′, what do the parentheses mean?	The carbon is double-bonded to the oxygen inside the parentheses.
Which functional group is –OH when attached to a carbon outside a carboxyl group?	Hydroxyl group.
Write the structural shorthand for a hydroxyl group.	–OH.
What property does a hydroxyl group usually add to a carbon-containing region?	Polarity, allowing favorable interactions with water.
How can a hydroxyl group interact with water?	By forming hydrogen bonds.
Is an ordinary alcohol –OH group charged as drawn?	No; it is polar but uncharged.
Which functional group appears repeatedly on a typical sugar?	Hydroxyl.
Which functional group on glycerol reacts with a fatty acid?	A hydroxyl group.

What happens to –COOH when it donates H⁺?	It becomes –COO⁻.
Why can a carboxyl group acquire a negative charge?	It can lose H⁺ while retaining the bonding electrons.
Which is negatively charged: –COOH or –COO⁻?	–COO⁻.
What is the name of the deprotonated form of a carboxyl group?	Carboxylate.
A hydrocarbon chain ends in –C(=O)–OH. Which end can donate H⁺?	The carboxyl end.
Which functional group occurs at the end of a fatty acid?	Carboxyl.
Which group of an amino acid supplies the carbonyl carbon of a peptide linkage?	The carboxyl group.
Does a C=O bond alone identify a carboxyl group?	No; a carboxyl group also has –OH or –O⁻ on that same carbon.
Identify the nitrogen-containing group in R–NH₂.	Amino group.
Write a neutral amino group attached to R.	R–NH₂.
What does R–NH₂ become when it accepts H⁺?	R–NH₃⁺.
Why can an amino group act as a base?	Its nitrogen can accept H⁺.
Which is positively charged: R–NH₂ or R–NH₃⁺?	R–NH₃⁺.
How does protonation of an amino group affect its interaction with water?	The resulting positive charge favors interaction with water.
Which group of an amino acid supplies nitrogen to a peptide linkage?	The amino group.
What atom surrounded by oxygens is a clue to a phosphate-containing group?	Phosphorus (P).
Give one common schematic form of a phosphate monoester with negative charges.	R–O–P(=O)(O⁻)–O⁻; protonation can vary.
Why can phosphate groups make a molecular region hydrophilic?	Their polar bonds and often negative charges interact favorably with water.
Are all phosphate-containing groups always drawn with exactly the same charge?	No; charge depends on protonation and the groups attached.
Which repeating region of DNA and RNA contains phosphate groups?	The sugar–phosphate backbone.
In a typical phospholipid, which region contains phosphate?	The polar head region.
Identify the functional group C=O.	Carbonyl.
Write the structural shorthand for a carbonyl group.	C=O.
Why is a carbonyl group polar?	Oxygen pulls the shared electrons more strongly than carbon.
Which end of a carbonyl bond has a partial negative charge?	The oxygen end.
Does the polarity of C=O mean that the group necessarily has a net charge?	No; partial charges differ from a net ionic charge.
Which feature do carboxyl, ester, and amide groups share?	A carbonyl group.
Identify the group –CH₃.	Methyl group.
Write the structural shorthand for a methyl group.	–CH₃.
Is a methyl group mainly polar or nonpolar?	Nonpolar.
A region contains mostly C–C and C–H bonds. Is it mainly polar or nonpolar?	Nonpolar.
Why is a long hydrocarbon chain hydrophobic?	Its mostly nonpolar bonds cannot form favorable hydrogen bonds with water.
Which region interacts more favorably with water: –CH₂–CH₂– or –CH₂–OH?	–CH₂–OH, because it contains a polar hydroxyl group.
Which region interacts more favorably with water: –COO⁻ or –CH₃?	–COO⁻, because its negative charge interacts with water.
Can an uncharged group still be hydrophilic?	Yes; polar groups such as hydroxyls can interact with water.
Does one hydroxyl guarantee that an entire molecule is highly water-soluble?	No; the size of its nonpolar region also matters.
Classify –COO⁻ as charged or uncharged and give the sign.	Charged; negative.
Classify –NH₃⁺ as charged or uncharged and give the sign.	Charged; positive.
Classify an alcohol –OH as charged or uncharged in its usual drawn form.	Uncharged.
What covalent linkage joins amino acids in a protein backbone?	Peptide bond, a type of amide linkage.
Which two functional groups react in the course model of peptide-bond formation?	A carboxyl group and an amino group.
Recognize R–C(=O)–NH–R′: what linkage is shown?	An amide linkage; it is a peptide linkage when joining amino-acid residues.
Write a typical peptide/amide linkage motif.	–C(=O)–NH–.
In –C(=O)–NH–, which bond is the peptide bond itself?	The bond between the carbonyl carbon and nitrogen.
Which class of macromolecule has repeated peptide bonds in its backbone?	Proteins.
What units does a peptide bond connect in proteins?	Amino-acid residues.
What net reaction type forms a peptide bond in the course's simplified model?	Condensation/dehydration.

What small molecule is consumed when a peptide bond is hydrolyzed?	Water.
What small molecule is released in the simplified condensation of two free amino acids?	Water.
Why is R–C(=O)–NH–R′ not a free amino group?	Its nitrogen is directly bonded to a carbonyl carbon as part of an amide.
Why is the –C(=O)–NH– part of a peptide not a free carboxyl group?	The carbonyl carbon is bonded to nitrogen instead of –OH or –O⁻.
How does R–NH₂ differ structurally from R–C(=O)–NH–R′?	Only the second has nitrogen directly attached to a carbonyl carbon.
Does every nitrogen atom in a protein belong to a free amino group?	No; many are part of peptide linkages.
Why do peptide bonds matter to protein structure at this course level?	They covalently join amino-acid residues into the backbone.
Identify the linkage R–C(=O)–O–R′.	Ester.
Write the structural motif of an ester linkage.	–C(=O)–O–.
What two functional groups react to form an ester in the course model?	A hydroxyl group and a carboxyl group.
Which linkage would you expect between glycerol and a fatty acid?	Ester linkage.
Which glycerol group participates in ester formation?	A hydroxyl group.
Which fatty-acid group participates in ester formation?	The carboxyl group.
What is the net reaction type for hydroxyl + carboxyl → ester + water?	Condensation/dehydration, specifically esterification.
What reaction breaks an ester linkage by adding water?	Hydrolysis.
What functional groups are restored by the course model of ester hydrolysis?	A hydroxyl group and a carboxyl group.
Which storage lipid contains three ester linkages between glycerol and fatty acids?	Triacylglycerol.
In a common glycerol-based phospholipid, what links a fatty acid to glycerol?	An ester linkage.
Why do ester linkages matter in triacylglycerols?	They covalently attach the fatty-acid chains to glycerol.
A diagram contains R–O–C(=O)–R′. Which linkage is present?	Ester; reversing its drawing direction does not change the linkage.
A diagram contains C=O, but no oxygen single-bonded to that carbon. Is that enough to identify an ester?	No; an ester requires the –C(=O)–O– connection.
How can you distinguish a free carboxyl group from an ester in a diagram?	A carboxyl has –C(=O)–OH/–O⁻; an ester has –C(=O)–O–R.
Identify R–C(=O)–S–R′.	Thioester.
Write the structural motif of a thioester.	–C(=O)–S–.
Which atom replaces the linking oxygen of an ester in a thioester?	Sulfur.
What feature is shared by ester and thioester linkages?	A carbonyl carbon bonded to the linking oxygen or sulfur.
Which is a thioester: R–S–R′ or R–C(=O)–S–R′?	R–C(=O)–S–R′; sulfur must be attached to the carbonyl carbon.
What introductory biological role can thioesters have?	They can carry acyl groups, the R–C(=O)– portion, in metabolic reactions.
Identify the phosphate attachment in R–O–P(=O)(O⁻)–O⁻.	A phosphoester linkage.
In a phosphoester, how is the carbon-containing group connected to phosphate?	Through an alcohol-derived oxygen: C–O–P.
What kind of group provides the alcohol-derived oxygen in a phosphoester?	A hydroxyl group.
Which two groups are joined in the simple condensation model of phosphoester formation?	An alcohol hydroxyl group and a phosphate –OH group.
What reaction type can cleave a phosphoester linkage using water?	Hydrolysis.
A nucleotide's sugar is attached to one phosphate through C–O–P. What linkage is that attachment?	Phosphoester.
How many alcohol-derived organic groups are ester-linked to the phosphate in a phosphate monoester?	One.
How many alcohol-derived organic groups are ester-linked to the phosphate in a phosphodiester?	Two.
Identify sugar–O–P(=O)(O⁻)–O–sugar.	A phosphodiester connection.
Which covalent linkage creates the DNA/RNA sugar–phosphate backbone?	Phosphodiester linkage.
What units are connected by phosphodiester linkages in DNA and RNA?	Nucleotide residues.
Which sugar-carbon positions are connected through phosphate in a normal nucleic-acid backbone?	The 3′ position of one sugar and the 5′ position of the next.
Does a 3′–5′ phosphodiester linkage directly join two sugar carbons with a C–C bond?	No; the connection passes through O–P–O.
How does a phosphodiester relate to phosphoester attachments?	One phosphate has two phosphoester attachments to organic groups.
What feature distinguishes a phosphodiester from a phosphate monoester in a diagram?	Two organic groups are attached through oxygens to the same phosphate instead of one.
Does any phosphate group in a drawing automatically identify a phosphodiester?	No; check for two ester attachments to organic groups.
What reaction type can break the nucleic-acid backbone by using water?	Hydrolysis of a phosphodiester linkage.
Which nucleic-acid group is required at the growing chain end to form the next phosphodiester linkage?	A usable 3′-OH.
Why does the 3′–5′ connection give a nucleic-acid strand directionality?	The strand has chemically different 5′ and 3′ ends.
Does a phosphodiester bond connect the paired bases across two DNA strands?	No; it connects successive nucleotides within one strand.
Which linkage joins monosaccharides in disaccharides and polysaccharides?	Glycosidic linkage.
What units does a glycosidic linkage connect in the carbohydrates in this course?	Monosaccharide residues.
What groups react in the simplified model of glycosidic-bond formation between sugars?	Hydroxyl groups on the two sugars, including the anomeric hydroxyl of one.
What reaction type joins two free sugars with net loss of water in the course model?	Condensation/dehydration.

In glucose polymers, what does the 1 in a (1→4) linkage identify?	Carbon 1 of one glucose residue.
In glucose polymers, what does the 4 in a (1→4) linkage identify?	Carbon 4 of the other glucose residue.
Does 1→4 mean that four glucose molecules are connected?	No; it identifies the linked carbon positions.
In α(1→6), which carbon of the second glucose is linked?	Carbon 6.
What does α or β specify in a glycosidic-linkage name?	The configuration at the participating anomeric carbon, carbon 1 in glucose.
In a standard D-glucose Haworth drawing, how is an α substituent at C1 oriented relative to the C5–CH₂OH group?	On the opposite side of the ring.
In a standard D-glucose Haworth drawing, how is a β substituent at C1 oriented relative to the C5–CH₂OH group?	On the same side of the ring.
Can the numbers (1→4) alone distinguish starch from cellulose?	No; the α versus β configuration also matters.

Which is an ether: CH₃–O–CH₃ or CH₃–C(=O)–O–CH₃?	CH₃–O–CH₃.
