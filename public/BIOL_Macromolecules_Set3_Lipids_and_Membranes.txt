Why are lipids not classified as conventional repeating-monomer polymers?	Their diverse structures do not generally consist of long chains of identical repeating monomers.
What two regions make up a fatty acid?	A hydrocarbon region and a carboxyl group.
Which region of a fatty acid is mainly nonpolar?	The hydrocarbon chain.
Which region of a fatty acid can ionize in water?	The carboxyl group.
Complete the equilibrium: COOH ⇌ ____ + H⁺.	COO⁻.
Does ionizing a fatty acid's carboxyl group make its hydrocarbon chain polar?	No; the chain remains mainly nonpolar.
What components make one triacylglycerol?	One glycerol and three fatty acids.
How many hydroxyl groups does glycerol have before esterification?	Three.
How many ester linkages attach fatty acids in one triacylglycerol?	Three.
What is the primary role of triacylglycerols in this unit?	Energy storage.
Which lipid combines glycerol with three fatty acids through ester linkages?	Triacylglycerol.
What net reaction joins glycerol hydroxyls to fatty-acid carboxyls?	Esterification by condensation/dehydration.
In the simplified formation of one triacylglycerol from glycerol and three free fatty acids, how many waters are released?	Three, one per ester linkage.
What are the products of complete triacylglycerol hydrolysis in the course model?	Glycerol and three fatty acids.
Can the three fatty-acid chains of a triacylglycerol vary in length?	Yes.
What does hydrophilic mean?	Interacts favorably with water.
What chemical features usually make a region hydrophilic?	Polar bonds or ionic charges.
What does hydrophobic mean?	Interacts poorly with water relative to polar or charged regions.
What chemical features usually make a region hydrophobic?	A largely nonpolar, uncharged structure.
What does amphipathic mean?	Having both hydrophilic and hydrophobic regions.
A molecule has a polar head and nonpolar tails. What term describes this arrangement?	Amphipathic.
Which course example is amphipathic: soap or a hydrocarbon-only oil molecule?	Soap.
Which part of a typical phospholipid faces water in a membrane?	The hydrophilic head region.
Which part of a typical phospholipid avoids exposure to water?	The hydrocarbon tails.
What components form the polar region of the course's glycerol-based phospholipid?	The polar head group, phosphate, and oxygen-containing glycerol region.
What backbone connects the head region and fatty-acid chains in a glycerol-based phospholipid?	Glycerol.
Which head-group component is named in phosphatidylcholine?	Choline.
How many fatty-acid tails does the typical glycerol-based phospholipid in this unit have?	Two.
What linkage attaches each fatty acid to glycerol in the course's typical phospholipid?	Ester linkage.
Can an overall electrically neutral phospholipid still have a hydrophilic head?	Yes; its head can contain polar bonds and opposite charges.
What kind of fatty-acid double bond commonly creates a kink?	A cis double bond.
Does every possible C=C arrangement necessarily create the same kink?	No; the course's bent-tail drawing represents a cis double bond.
Compared with straight tails, how do kinked tails affect close packing?	They make close packing more difficult.
Why is an isolated single phospholipid sheet unstable when water surrounds it on both sides?	Its tails would be exposed to water on one side.
In a bilayer, where do the hydrophilic heads point?	Toward water on both outer surfaces.
In a bilayer, where do the hydrophobic tails point?	Inward, toward one another and away from water.
What is a leaflet?	One of the two phospholipid layers making up a bilayer.
How many leaflets make one bilayer?	Two.
What is a liposome?	A closed phospholipid bilayer enclosing an aqueous cavity.
What is inside a liposome's central cavity?	Water and potentially dissolved substances.
Is a liposome's central cavity the same as its hydrophobic bilayer interior?	No; the cavity is aqueous, while the tails occupy the bilayer itself.
What is a micelle?	An aggregate with hydrophilic heads outside and hydrophobic tails packed inward.
Does a typical micelle have an aqueous central cavity?	No; its core is hydrophobic.
Which course aggregate has an aqueous cavity: a micelle or a liposome?	A liposome.
Which molecular shape favors micelles in the course notes?	A bulky hydrophilic head with a single hydrophobic tail.
Which arrangement is favored by the typical two-tailed phospholipids in this unit?	A bilayer.
Why can closing a bilayer into a liposome be favorable?	It removes exposed edges where hydrophobic tails could contact water.
A circular cross-section has one layer of heads outside and tails filling the center. Identify the aggregate.	Micelle.
A circular cross-section has heads facing both the outside water and a water-filled center. Identify the aggregate.	Liposome.
Does one lipid bilayer mean two separate membranes?	No; its two leaflets form one membrane bilayer.
What does a mitochondrial double membrane mean?	Two separate bilayers with an intermembrane space between them.
How many leaflets are present across two separate bilayers?	Four.
Which two course organelle groups have double membranes?	Mitochondria and plastids.
Name a course organelle surrounded by a single membrane bilayer.	ER, Golgi, or lysosome.
What membrane property helps maintain cellular homeostasis?	Selective permeability.
What creates the main barrier to ions in a lipid bilayer?	Its hydrophobic interior.
Which passes through a lipid bilayer more easily: O₂ or Na⁺?	O₂; it is small and nonpolar.
Can water cross a lipid bilayer at all without a channel?	Yes, to a limited extent; aquaporins enable much faster movement.
Why do many polar solutes need membrane transport proteins?	The hydrophobic bilayer interior strongly limits their direct passage.
What two membrane components together determine selective permeability?	The lipid bilayer and membrane proteins.
What kind of protein forms a selective aqueous passage across a membrane?	A channel protein.
What kind of transport protein binds a solute and changes shape to move it?	A carrier protein.
What are aquaporins specialized to transport?	Water.
Does movement through a membrane protein automatically mean active transport?	No; facilitated diffusion also uses proteins.
What distinguishes simple from facilitated diffusion?	Simple diffusion crosses the lipid bilayer; facilitated diffusion uses a membrane protein.
Does facilitated diffusion directly require ATP?	No; it moves substances down their gradient.
At diffusion equilibrium, do molecules stop moving?	No; movement continues in both directions with no net movement.
What distinguishes active transport from passive transport?	Active transport uses energy to move a substance against its gradient.
Where does primary active transport obtain energy directly?	ATP, in the course examples.
Where does secondary active transport obtain its immediate energy?	An ion moving down its electrochemical gradient.
Why can secondary active transport be active even without direct ATP use?	It couples downhill ion movement to uphill movement of another solute.
What two differences make up an electrochemical gradient?	Ion concentration difference and electrical potential difference.
What is osmosis?	Net water movement across a selectively permeable membrane.
With equal pressure and impermeant solute, toward which side does water move by osmosis?	Toward the side with higher effective solute concentration.
What happens to an animal cell in a hypotonic solution?	Water enters and the cell swells; severe swelling can cause lysis.
What happens to an animal cell in a hypertonic solution?	Water leaves and the cell shrinks.
What happens to net water movement in an isotonic solution?	There is no net water movement, although water still moves both ways.
Why do ion pumps help cells regulate their volume?	Ion concentrations affect osmotic water movement.
What does a contractile vacuole do in a freshwater protist?	Collects and expels excess water.
What is turgor pressure?	Outward pressure of cell contents against the plant cell wall.
How does a plant cell wall limit swelling?	It resists expansion and pushes back against turgor pressure.
What protein framework helps maintain cell shape?	The cytoskeleton.
