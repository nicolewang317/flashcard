Is an ordinary alcohol –OH group charged as drawn?	No; it is polar but uncharged.
Why does a carboxyl group become negatively charged after donating H⁺?	It loses H⁺ but retains the bonding electrons, forming carboxylate (–COO⁻).
Why can an amino group act as a base?	Its nitrogen accepts H⁺: R–NH₂ becomes R–NH₃⁺.
Why can phosphate groups make a molecular region hydrophilic?	Their polar bonds and often negative charges interact favorably with water.
Are all phosphate-containing groups always drawn with exactly the same charge?	No; charge depends on protonation and the groups attached.
Why is the oxygen end of a carbonyl partially negative?	Oxygen attracts the shared electrons more strongly than carbon.
Does the polarity of C=O mean that the group necessarily has a net charge?	No; partial charges differ from a net ionic charge.
Why is a long hydrocarbon chain hydrophobic?	Its mostly nonpolar bonds cannot form favorable hydrogen bonds with water.
Which interacts more favorably with water: –CH₂–CH₂– or –CH₂–OH, and why?	–CH₂–OH: its polar hydroxyl can form hydrogen bonds with water.
Which region interacts more favorably with water: –COO⁻ or –CH₃?	–COO⁻, because its negative charge interacts with water.
Does one hydroxyl guarantee that an entire molecule is highly water-soluble?	No; the size of its nonpolar region also matters.
Using the lecture’s electronegativity ordering, why are C–H regions treated as nonpolar?	C and H have similar electronegativities, so their bonding electrons are shared relatively evenly.
Classify the attraction between Na⁺ and the oxygen end of a water molecule.	Ion–permanent dipole (ion–PD).
A nearby ion distorts the electron cloud of a nonpolar group. What interaction results?	Ion–induced dipole (ion–ID).
A polar carbonyl distorts the electron cloud of a neighboring nonpolar group. Classify the interaction.	Permanent dipole–induced dipole (PD–ID).
What weak attraction can occur between two nonpolar groups through fluctuating electron distributions?	Induced dipole–induced dipole (ID–ID), or London dispersion attraction.
Two neutral carbonyl groups align with δ⁺ carbon near δ⁻ oxygen. What interaction can occur?	Permanent dipole–permanent dipole (PD–PD).
What strongest-to-weakest order is used in the lecture’s simplified interaction scheme?	Ion–ion > ion–PD > hydrogen bond > other PD–PD > ion–ID > PD–ID > ID–ID.
Can two permanent dipoles repel even though each molecule is neutral overall?	Yes. Orientation can bring like partial charges together; opposite partial charges favor attraction.
Why is the induced-dipole contribution attractive in the lecture model?	The electron cloud shifts so the nearer induced charge is opposite the inducing charge.
What distinguishes a covalent bond from the noncovalent interactions in the lecture?	Covalent bonding shares electrons; noncovalent interactions act between charge distributions without that covalent link.
