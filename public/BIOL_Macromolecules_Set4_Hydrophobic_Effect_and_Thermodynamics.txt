About what fraction of cell mass is water in the course notes?	About 70%.
What noncovalent interaction do water molecules form with one another?	Hydrogen bonds.
Does calling water a universal solvent mean it dissolves every substance well?	No; nonpolar substances often dissolve poorly.
What effect is central to spontaneous bilayer assembly in water?	The hydrophobic effect.
Write the Gibbs free-energy equation used in this unit.	ΔG = ΔH − TΔS.
What does ΔG represent?	The change in Gibbs free energy.
What does ΔH represent?	The enthalpy change; heat absorbed or released at constant pressure.
What does ΔS represent?	The entropy change of the system being considered.
What temperature scale is required in ΔG = ΔH − TΔS?	Kelvin.
What does ΔG < 0 mean at constant temperature and pressure?	The process is thermodynamically favorable in the stated direction.
What does ΔG > 0 mean for the stated process under those conditions?	It is thermodynamically unfavorable without coupling or changed conditions.
What does ΔG = 0 indicate at equilibrium?	No net thermodynamic driving force in either direction.
Does spontaneous mean instantaneous or fast?	No; spontaneity describes thermodynamic favorability, not rate.
Does spontaneous bilayer assembly require zero molecular motion?	No; molecular motion allows lipids to rearrange.
What happens to nearby water when an isolated hydrophobic tail is exposed in the course model?	Water becomes more ordered and its motion is restricted.
How does ordering water around exposed hydrophobic tails affect water entropy?	It lowers water entropy.
When many hydrophobic tails aggregate, what happens to the total tail surface exposed to water?	It decreases.
Why are water molecules released when hydrophobic tails aggregate?	Less hydrophobic surface remains exposed, so fewer waters must organize around it.
What happens to the freedom of released water molecules?	They become freer to move and arrange themselves.
What is the sign of ΔS_water during aggregation in the lecture model?	Positive.
What is the sign of ΔS_lipids during ordered bilayer assembly in the lecture model?	Negative.
Why can lipid entropy decrease during assembly?	Lipids become more constrained in the organized aggregate.
In the lecture model, which entropy change is larger in magnitude during favorable assembly?	The increase in water entropy exceeds the decrease in lipid entropy.
When adding water and lipid entropy changes, what is the sign of total ΔS in the lecture model?	Positive.
Why is looking only at lipid order insufficient to judge bilayer formation?	The surrounding water contributes to the system's entropy change too.
If ΔS is positive and T is positive, what is the sign of −TΔS?	Negative.
How can an increase in total entropy favor bilayer assembly?	It makes −TΔS more negative, lowering ΔG.
Does a positive ΔS alone guarantee a negative ΔG?	No; ΔH must also be included in ΔG = ΔH − TΔS.
What is the lecture's main explanation for hydrophobic-tail aggregation?	Aggregation releases ordered water, increasing water entropy enough to favor assembly.
Do tails aggregate because water forms covalent bonds between them?	No; assembly is driven by noncovalent interactions and the hydrophobic effect.
Are strong special bonds between hydrophobic tails required to explain their aggregation?	No; the water-entropy contribution is central in the course model.
Does hydrophobic mean that water and a hydrocarbon have absolutely no interactions?	No; the interactions are less favorable than those available to polar or charged regions.
Why do phospholipid heads remain exposed to water during assembly?	Their polar or charged groups interact favorably with water.
Why do tails become buried during assembly?	Burial reduces hydrophobic surface exposed to water.
What connects amphipathic structure to the orientation of lipids in a bilayer?	Polar heads favor water contact while nonpolar tails become sheltered inside.
A bilayer forms and the lipids become more ordered. Has the second law necessarily been violated?	No; water and the surroundings must also be included in the entropy accounting.
What is the appropriate system for the lecture's lipid-versus-water entropy comparison?	The lipids together with the surrounding water.
If ΔS_lipids = −20 J/K and ΔS_water = +50 J/K, what is their combined ΔS?	+30 J/K.
If ΔS_lipids = −40 J/K and ΔS_water = +15 J/K, does their combined entropy increase?	No; the combined ΔS is −25 J/K.
If T = 300 K and ΔS = +0.020 kJ/K, what is TΔS?	+6 kJ.
If ΔH = +2 kJ and TΔS = +6 kJ, what is ΔG?	−4 kJ; favorable in the stated direction.
If ΔH = +8 kJ and TΔS = +6 kJ, what is ΔG?	+2 kJ; unfavorable in the stated direction.
Why must ΔH and TΔS use compatible units before subtraction?	Both terms must be expressed in the same energy units.
Assuming ΔH and positive ΔS remain constant, how does increasing T affect ΔG?	It lowers ΔG because −TΔS becomes more negative.
Does the lecture's hydrophobic-effect model require bilayer formation to release heat?	No; a sufficiently favorable entropy term can outweigh positive ΔH.
What would exposing more hydrophobic tail area do to nearby water in the course model?	It would require more water ordering and reduce water entropy.
Why does a bilayer's exposed edge tend to be unfavorable in water?	It exposes hydrophobic tails, promoting water ordering around them.
Why is the hydrophobic effect weaker as an explanation if no water is present?	The water-ordering and water-release mechanism no longer applies.
Must an aggregate's molecules stay completely motionless for it to be stable?	No; thermodynamic stability is compatible with ongoing molecular motion.
Is forming a bilayer the same chemical process as synthesizing a phospholipid?	No; assembly rearranges existing lipids, while synthesis forms covalent linkages.
What changes during esterification that need not change during bilayer assembly?	Covalent bonds change during esterification.
An exam answer says “lipids order, so entropy decreases and assembly cannot occur.” What did it omit?	The entropy increase of water released from exposed hydrophobic surfaces.
An exam answer says “ΔG is negative because the tails want to hide.” What mechanism should replace that wording?	Tail burial releases ordered water; the resulting entropy contribution lowers ΔG.
After tails cluster, fewer waters contact them. Does that mean the released water disappears?	No; it returns to the surrounding bulk water.
What is the immediate link between reduced tail exposure and increased water entropy?	Fewer water molecules remain constrained around hydrophobic surfaces.
Does ΔG < 0 prove that ΔS_lipids > 0?	No; ΔG depends on enthalpy and the entropy change of the full system.
Which term in ΔG = ΔH − TΔS captures the lecture's main water-release contribution?	The entropy term, −TΔS.
