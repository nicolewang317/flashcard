What membrane property helps maintain cellular homeostasis?	Selective permeability.
Which passes through a lipid bilayer more easily: O₂ or Na⁺?	O₂; it is small and nonpolar.
Why does adding aquaporins increase water transport across a membrane?	Water can cross the lipid bilayer slowly; aquaporin channels provide a much faster pathway.
Why do ions usually need transport proteins to cross a lipid bilayer?	The hydrophobic interior is energetically unfavorable for charged, hydrated ions.
What two membrane components together determine selective permeability?	The lipid bilayer and membrane proteins.
How does a carrier move a solute differently from a channel?	A carrier binds the solute and changes conformation; a channel provides a selective pore.
Does movement through a membrane protein automatically mean active transport?	No; facilitated diffusion also uses proteins.
What distinguishes simple from facilitated diffusion?	Simple diffusion crosses the lipid bilayer; facilitated diffusion uses a membrane protein.
At diffusion equilibrium, do molecules stop moving?	No; movement continues in both directions with no net movement.
What distinguishes active transport from passive transport?	Active transport uses energy to move a substance against its gradient.
Where does primary active transport obtain energy directly?	ATP, in the course examples.
Why can secondary active transport be active even without direct ATP use?	It couples downhill ion movement to uphill movement of another solute.
What two differences make up an electrochemical gradient?	Ion concentration difference and electrical potential difference.
A membrane passes water but not solute. Side A has more solute than B, with equal pressure. Which way is net water flow?	From B to A, toward the higher concentration of nonpenetrating solute.
An animal cell enters a hypotonic solution. What happens to its volume?	Water enters by osmosis; the cell swells and may lyse.
Why do ion pumps help cells regulate their volume?	Ion concentrations affect osmotic water movement.
What does a contractile vacuole do in a freshwater protist?	Collects and expels excess water.
Why does a plant cell wall limit osmotic swelling?	As water enters, the wall resists expansion; rising turgor pressure opposes further net water entry.
Why does glucose cross a protein-free lipid bilayer much less readily than O₂?	Glucose is larger and has many polar hydroxyl groups; entering the hydrophobic core is unfavorable.
Two fixed 1-L compartments contain 8 mM and 2 mM of a freely permeating neutral solute. With no reactions, what concentration is reached on each side?	5 mM on each side: total solute is conserved and concentrations become equal.
For neutral glucose transported by GLUT, what happens to net flux if the glucose concentration gradient reverses?	Net glucose flux reverses; GLUT mediates facilitated diffusion down the gradient.
Opening a gated ion channel allows flux. What determines its net direction?	The ion’s electrochemical gradient; opening the gate does not provide energy for uphill pumping.
In one normal Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase cycle, what crosses the membrane per ATP hydrolyzed?	3 Na⁺ move out and 2 K⁺ move in.
A pump moves 3 Na⁺ out and 2 K⁺ in per cycle. What is its net charge transfer?	One positive charge moves outward per cycle.
Na⁺ and glucose move together into a cell. Is this symport or antiport?	Symport: the two substances cross in the same direction; antiport means opposite directions.
A positive ion is more concentrated outside, but the inside is strongly positive. Can concentration alone predict net channel flux?	No. The inward chemical drive competes with the outward electrical drive; their combined effect determines net flux.
After Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase inhibition, the inward Na⁺ driving force gradually collapses. Why does uphill glucose uptake by Na⁺–glucose symport weaken?	The cotransporter loses the downhill Na⁺ energy that powers uphill glucose transport.
Which can maintain uphill glucose transport: GLUT or a Na⁺–glucose cotransporter supplied with a sufficient inward Na⁺ gradient?	The Na⁺–glucose cotransporter uses ion-gradient energy; GLUT alone permits only downhill glucose flux.
