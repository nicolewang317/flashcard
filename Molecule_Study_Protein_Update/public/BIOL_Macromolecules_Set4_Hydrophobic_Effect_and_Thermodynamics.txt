What noncovalent interaction do water molecules form with one another?	Hydrogen bonds.
In ΔG = ΔH − TΔS, what competes with the enthalpy change?	The entropy contribution −TΔS; T is absolute temperature and ΔS is the system’s entropy change.
What temperature scale is required in ΔG = ΔH − TΔS?	Kelvin.
At constant temperature and pressure, what does ΔG < 0 tell you?	The process is thermodynamically favorable in the forward direction; it says nothing about its speed.
Does spontaneous mean instantaneous or fast?	No; spontaneity describes thermodynamic favorability, not rate.
In the lecture model, why does water near exposed nonpolar tails have lower entropy?	Its possible arrangements and motion are more constrained around the nonpolar surfaces.
Why does burying hydrophobic tails increase water entropy in the lecture model?	Less nonpolar surface contacts water, releasing constrained water molecules into the bulk.
Why can lipid entropy decrease during assembly?	Lipids become more constrained in the organized aggregate.
How can bilayer formation be favored even though lipid entropy decreases?	The increase in water entropy can outweigh the decrease in lipid entropy.
How does a positive ΔS affect ΔG when ΔH and T are fixed?	It makes −TΔS negative and lowers ΔG.
Does a positive ΔS alone guarantee a negative ΔG?	No; ΔH must also be included in ΔG = ΔH − TΔS.
Does hydrophobic mean that water and a hydrocarbon have absolutely no interactions?	No; the interactions are less favorable than those available to polar or charged regions.
At constant T and P, how does a spontaneous process with ΔG < 0 relate to the second law?	The entropy of the system plus surroundings increases; the system alone need not gain entropy.
Lipids change entropy by −20 J/K and water by +50 J/K. What is their combined change?	+30 J/K: the water’s increase outweighs the lipid decrease.
At 300 K, ΔH = +2 kJ and ΔS = +20 J/K. Calculate ΔG.	ΔG = 2 − 300 × 0.020 = −4 kJ; the process is favorable.
Assuming ΔH and positive ΔS remain constant, how does increasing T affect ΔG?	It lowers ΔG because −TΔS becomes more negative.
Why is the hydrophobic effect weaker as an explanation if no water is present?	The water-ordering and water-release mechanism no longer applies.
Must an aggregate's molecules stay completely motionless for it to be stable?	No; thermodynamic stability is compatible with ongoing molecular motion.
Is forming a bilayer the same chemical process as synthesizing a phospholipid?	No; assembly rearranges existing lipids, while synthesis forms covalent linkages.
