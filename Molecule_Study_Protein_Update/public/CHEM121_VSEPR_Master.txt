What does A represent in AXₓEₑ notation?	The central atom.
What does X count in the supplied VSEPR tables?	Atoms attached to the central atom.
What does E count in the supplied VSEPR tables?	Lone pairs on the central atom.
A central atom has four bonded atoms and one lone pair. How many electron domains determine its parent geometry?	Five domains (X + E = 4 + 1).
Does the molecular shape name include the positions of lone pairs?	No. It describes the attached atoms; lone pairs still influence their arrangement.
When do the parent shape and molecular shape have the same name in these tables?	When E = 0: every parent position is occupied by an attached atom.
In VSEPR domain counting, is a double bond one region or two around A?	One bonding region; multiple bonds connect A to one attached atom in the table’s X count.
E + X = 2. What is the parent shape?	Linear.
E + X = 3. What is the parent shape?	Trigonal planar.

How do solid wedges and dashed wedges differ in a perspective bond drawing?	A solid wedge points toward you; a dashed wedge points away.
Does rotating a perspective drawing change the molecular shape?	No. The relative three-dimensional arrangement stays the same.

Why does the word “bent” not uniquely specify the parent geometry?	AX₂E is trigonal planar in electron geometry; AX₂E₂ is tetrahedral. Lone pairs count toward the parent shape.
Why is AX₄E₂ square planar rather than tetrahedral?	Its six domains have an octahedral parent; two opposite lone pairs leave four atoms in a square.
A molecule has X = 3. Is that enough to choose between trigonal planar, trigonal pyramidal, and T-shaped?	No. You also need E and therefore the parent shape.
Why do lone pairs prefer equatorial sites in a trigonal-bipyramidal electron geometry?	An equatorial site has only two 90° interactions; an axial site has three.

What ideal bond angles occur in pentagonal-bipyramidal AX₇?	72° and 144° between equatorial bonds; 90° axial–equatorial; 180° axial–axial.
Derive the angle between adjacent equatorial bonds in ideal pentagonal-bipyramidal geometry.	360° ÷ 5 = 72° because five equatorial bonds are evenly spaced.
Why should 109.5° not be treated as the exact bond angle of every AX₃E or AX₂E₂ molecule?	109.5° is the ideal tetrahedral domain angle. Lone pairs repel more strongly and usually compress bond angles.
