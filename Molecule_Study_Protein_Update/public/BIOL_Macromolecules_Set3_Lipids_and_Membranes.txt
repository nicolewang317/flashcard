Why are lipids not classified as conventional repeating-monomer polymers?	Their diverse structures do not generally consist of long chains of identical repeating monomers.
What two regions make up a fatty acid?	A hydrocarbon region and a carboxyl group.
Does ionizing a fatty acid's carboxyl group make its hydrocarbon chain polar?	No; the chain remains mainly nonpolar.
How many hydroxyl groups does glycerol have before esterification?	Three.
How many ester linkages attach fatty acids in one triacylglycerol?	Three.
What is the primary role of triacylglycerols in this unit?	Energy storage.
In the simplified formation of one triacylglycerol from glycerol and three free fatty acids, how many waters are released?	Three, one per ester linkage.
What products result from complete hydrolysis of one triacylglycerol in the course model?	Glycerol and three fatty acids (or their carboxylates, depending on pH).
What chemical features usually make a region hydrophilic?	Polar bonds or ionic charges.
A molecule has a polar head and nonpolar tails. What term describes this arrangement?	Amphipathic.
Which parts make the head region of phosphatidylcholine hydrophilic?	Its charged phosphate and choline groups, plus nearby polar oxygen-containing bonds.
What backbone connects the head region and fatty-acid chains in a glycerol-based phospholipid?	Glycerol.
Which head-group component is named in phosphatidylcholine?	Choline.
How many fatty-acid tails does the typical glycerol-based phospholipid in this unit have?	Two.
Can an overall electrically neutral phospholipid still have a hydrophilic head?	Yes; its head can contain polar bonds and opposite charges.
What kind of fatty-acid double bond commonly creates a kink?	A cis double bond.
Compared with straight tails, how do kinked tails affect close packing?	They make close packing more difficult.
Why is an isolated single phospholipid sheet unstable when water surrounds it on both sides?	Its tails would be exposed to water on one side.
How are phospholipid heads and tails oriented in a bilayer surrounded by water?	Heads face water on both sides; tails face inward, away from water.
What is a leaflet?	One of the two phospholipid layers making up a bilayer.
Which course aggregate has an aqueous cavity: a micelle or a liposome?	A liposome.
Why do single-tail amphiphiles with bulky heads often favor micelles over bilayers?	Their cone-like shape packs well around a curved, tail-filled core.
Why can closing a bilayer into a liposome be favorable?	It removes exposed edges where hydrophobic tails could contact water.
A circular cross-section has one layer of heads outside and tails filling the center. Identify the aggregate.	Micelle.
A circular cross-section has heads facing both the outside water and a water-filled center. Identify the aggregate.	Liposome.
How many bilayers make up the mitochondrial double membrane?	Two separate bilayers, with an intermembrane space between them (four leaflets total).
Does the nuclear envelope contain one bilayer or two?	Two bilayers: the inner and outer nuclear membranes. The outer membrane is continuous with the ER.
Name a course organelle surrounded by a single membrane bilayer.	ER, Golgi, or lysosome.
What membrane property helps maintain cellular homeostasis?	Selective permeability.
Which passes through a lipid bilayer more easily: O₂ or Na⁺?	O₂; it is small and nonpolar.
Why does adding aquaporins increase water transport across a membrane?	Water can cross the lipid bilayer slowly; aquaporin channels provide a much faster pathway.
Why do ions usually need transport proteins to cross a lipid bilayer?	The hydrophobic interior is energetically unfavorable for charged, hydrated ions.
What two membrane components together determine selective permeability?	The lipid bilayer and membrane proteins.
How does a carrier move a solute differently from a channel?	A carrier binds the solute and changes conformation; a channel provides a selective pore.
Does movement through a membrane protein automatically mean active transport?	No; facilitated diffusion also uses proteins.
What distinguishes simple from facilitated diffusion?	Simple diffusion crosses the lipid bilayer; facilitated diffusion uses a membrane protein.
At diffusion equilibrium, do molecules stop moving?	No; movement continues in both directions with no net movement.
What distinguishes active transport from passive transport?	Active transport uses energy to move a substance against its gradient.
Where does primary active transport obtain energy directly?	ATP, in the course examples.
Why can secondary active transport be active even without direct ATP use?	It couples downhill ion movement to uphill movement of another solute.
What two differences make up an electrochemical gradient?	Ion concentration difference and electrical potential difference.
A membrane passes water but not solute. Side A has more solute than B, with equal pressure. Which way is net water flow?	From B to A, toward the higher concentration of nonpenetrating solute.
An animal cell enters a hypotonic solution. What happens to its volume?	Water enters by osmosis; the cell swells and may lyse.
Why do ion pumps help cells regulate their volume?	Ion concentrations affect osmotic water movement.
What does a contractile vacuole do in a freshwater protist?	Collects and expels excess water.
Why does a plant cell wall limit osmotic swelling?	As water enters, the wall resists expansion; rising turgor pressure opposes further net water entry.
What protein framework helps maintain cell shape?	The cytoskeleton.
