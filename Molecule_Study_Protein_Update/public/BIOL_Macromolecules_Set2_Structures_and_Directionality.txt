What three components make a nucleotide?	A phosphate group, a pentose sugar, and a nitrogenous base.
Which base differs between DNA and RNA?	DNA uses thymine (T); RNA uses uracil (U). Both use A, C, and G.
How do ribose and deoxyribose differ at the 2′ carbon?	Ribose has 2′-OH; deoxyribose has 2′-H.
Which pentose carbon is attached to the nitrogenous base?	1′.
Which pentose carbon bears the OH needed at the growing end of a nucleic-acid strand?	3′.
Which numbered pentose carbon lies outside the ring and is commonly attached to phosphate?	5′.
What does the prime mark in 3′ indicate in this context?	Sugar-carbon numbering, distinguished from numbering in the base.
How can you identify the two ends of the usual textbook nucleic-acid strand?	The 3′ end has a free 3′-OH; the 5′ end has the terminal 5′ position, commonly bearing phosphate.
Why is nucleic-acid synthesis described as 5′ → 3′?	New nucleotides attach to the growing strand’s 3′-OH, extending its 3′ end.
If the growing terminal nucleotide lacks a usable 3′-OH, what happens to normal extension?	It stops because the next backbone linkage cannot form there.
One DNA strand runs 5′ → 3′ along a given direction. How does its partner run along that same direction?	3′ → 5′: the strands are antiparallel.
What does polymer polarity mean when discussing 5′ and 3′ ends?	The polymer has chemically different ends and therefore a direction.
Are nitrogenous bases the repeating links of the sugar–phosphate backbone?	No; they attach to the sugars and project from the backbone.
Which part of an amino acid varies and determines many of its properties?	The R group, or side chain.
In H₂N–CH(R)–COOH, which substituent varies among amino acids?	The R group attached to the α-carbon.
An amino acid has –NH₃⁺, –COO⁻, and an uncharged R group. What is its net charge?	Zero: the +1 and −1 charges cancel, although both groups are charged.
How do you distinguish the N-terminus from the C-terminus in a peptide backbone?	The N-terminus has the free backbone amino group; the C-terminus has the free backbone carboxyl group.
To which end are new amino acids added during normal protein synthesis?	The C-terminal end.
A peptide is drawn with its free backbone carboxyl group on the left. At which end is the next residue added?	At the left-hand C-terminus; chemical identity determines direction, not page orientation.
How many peptide bonds join 10 amino acids in one unbranched, noncyclic chain?	9 peptide bonds (n − 1).
Does an amino group on a side chain define the N-terminus?	No; the N-terminus is the free amino end of the backbone.
Which disaccharide is made from two glucose units?	Maltose.
Complete hydrolysis produces glucose and galactose from one course disaccharide. Which one?	Lactose.
Complete hydrolysis produces glucose and fructose from one course disaccharide. Which one?	Sucrose.
Which polysaccharide is used for glucose storage in plants?	Starch.
What is cellulose's main course-level biological role?	Structural support in plant cell walls.
What linkage difference distinguishes starch from cellulose even though both contain glucose?	Starch has α(1→4) main-chain linkages; cellulose has β(1→4) linkages.
A plant glucose polymer has α(1→4) chains and α(1→6) branches. Identify it.	Amylopectin.
An enzyme recognizes α(1→4) linkages. Does that guarantee it can cleave β(1→4)?	No; the different geometry may prevent recognition.
An enzyme cleaves only α(1→4) bonds in amylopectin. Which branch-point linkage remains uncleaved by it?	α(1→6).
